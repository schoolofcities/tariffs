A city and province level analysis

Similar to our ranking charts for direct exposure to tariffs, we have built a tool for job losses. This comes from a Multi-Regional Input Output (MRIO) model built by Richard DiFrancesco.

A model to understand the impact of U.S. tariffs on Canadian cities

To understand the potential Canadian job losses due to U.S. tariffs, we’ve developed an interactive tool (below) derived from an MRIO model that allows users to explore how specific U.S. tariffs—on automotive, steel, lumber, aluminum, and more—could affect Canadians. The tool provides metrics on the number and proportion of jobs and businesses potentially exposed to tariffs (see also our neighbourhood mapping tool).

Results are based on tariffs as of September 1, 2026.

LOREM IPSUM

Policy implications?

  • Diversify local economies to reduce dependence on vulnerable sectors
  • Support businesses in accessing new markets and supply chains.
  • Invest in workforce development to build resilience in the face of trade uncertainty.

As trade tensions persist, Canadian policymakers must ensure that cities—especially smaller and specialized ones—are equipped to adapt and thrive.

Data sources list:

Estimates in these charts were created by combining data from the following sources:

  • Canadian Business Registry (Statistics Canada)
  • Canadian Chamber of Commerce
  • Canadian Census of Population (Statistics Canada)
  • Canadian International Merchandise Trade Web Application (Statistics Canada Catalogue No. 71-607-X2021004)
  • Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (United States International Trade Commission )
